Machine learning has changed empirical finance \citep{gu2020empirical}, and tail-risk forecasting is a natural candidate. Expected Shortfall (ES), the Basel market-risk measure, is jointly elicitable with Value-at-Risk (VaR) \citep{fissler2016higher}. A neural network can therefore be trained directly on a strictly consistent loss \citep{patton2019dynamic}. In a companion paper \citep{agrawal2026learning} we found that such networks match, but do not beat, the best econometric models when both see a market's own return history, and that they add information to forecast combinations. That leaves open a question with practical and economic content: is the bottleneck the \emph{architecture}, or the \emph{information}?

We answer it with a source of information that is available to every risk manager and largely ignored by univariate models: what other markets did overnight. Because markets trade in sequence across time zones, a risk manager in Tokyo or Taipei sets the morning VaR after the U.S.\ and European sessions have closed. Volatility spills over across markets \citep{engle1990meteor,hamao1990correlations,lin1994do,diebold2009measuring,diebold2012better}, and during crashes it may do so non-linearly \citep{forbes2002no}. Learning such non-linear, state-dependent transmission is exactly the kind of task deep learning is supposed to be good at.

We construct a pre-open information set that is free of look-ahead by design (Section~\ref{sec:info}). We then compare three ways of using it: a linear GJR-GARCH-X with skewed-$t$ innovations, an attention-based contagion network, and a neural news-impact GARCH. To guard against the forking-paths problem that afflicts machine-learning studies, the design is pre-registered. Models are developed on twelve indices, frozen in a public, time-stamped commit, and then evaluated exactly once on twelve further indices that no model had seen.

\paragraph{Findings.} First, cross-market information is valuable. Two news terms in a GARCH variance equation improve tail-risk forecasts at every level, with Diebold--Mariano statistics of about $-3.9$ in both samples. The gains are largest for Asian markets, consistent with overnight transmission from West to East. Second, the networks do not extract this information. None of the neural designs beats the linear model, and an ablation shows that the contagion network gains essentially nothing from the news that the linear model turns into a large improvement. Third, forecast combinations that include both families are the most accurate, and the companion paper's combination result replicates on the new markets ($t=-7.1$).

\paragraph{Contributions.} (i) A look-ahead-free, pre-open information set for daily tail-risk forecasting across time zones. (ii) Two theoretical results for neural tail-risk models: a composite FZ0+QLIKE score that is strictly consistent and more efficient for learning dynamics (Proposition~\ref{prop:composite}), and a neural news-impact GARCH with provable stability and exact nesting of GJR-GARCH (Proposition~\ref{prop:ng}). (iii) Out-of-sample evidence, under a pre-registered protocol, that in this setting information and inductive bias matter more than architecture, together with the mechanism behind this: tail-focused losses learn dynamics inefficiently. (iv) Evidence on the direction of international tail-risk transmission.
